Combating Opioid Addiction

Fighting the opioid epidemic through accessible and reliable analysis.

Opioids represent a broad category of compounds that can have a variety of applications.

One major application of opioids is the management of moderate to severe pain. These drugs have high addiction potentials, however, making misuse of these compounds a cause for concern. There can be serious medical consequences to opioid intoxication or overdose.

Since 1999, the number of opioid-involved deaths has increased dramatically. In 2023, approximately 76% of all drug overdose deaths involved opioids, with the number of deaths related to synthetic opioids (primarily the illicitly manufactured fentanyl) recorded at 72,776.

There are over 100 different types of opioids, which can be ground into three different categories:

  • Natural: Substances made from poppy seeds (also known as opiates)
  • Semi-synthetic: Substances created in a laboratory using natural opioids
  • Synthetic: Substances created in a laboratory without any natural ingredients

Synthetic opioids, such as fentanyl, can be up to 50 times stronger than the semi-synthetic heroin and 100 times stronger than the natural opioid morphine. With so many different compounds, it is important to have a sensitive and specific identification system that can quantify and differentiate between opioid molecules.
